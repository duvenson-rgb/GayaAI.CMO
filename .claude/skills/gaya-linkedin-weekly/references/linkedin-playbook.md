# LinkedIn playbook (condensed)

Distilled from the "LinkedIn GTM Top Voice" project files (Christian Di Bratto's 1, One, 1 newsletter,
creator transcripts and the Writing Humanizer). Source files stay the reference; this is what drafting uses.

## 1. Every post moves the reader to Know, Like or Trust Gaya more
- **Know** (top of funnel): opinions, market takes, personal/team stories. Reach, no CTA.
- **Like** (middle): how-tos, mistakes, process breakdowns featuring the product.
- **Trust** (bottom): customer stories, proof, product truth, objection answers. Soft CTA allowed.
If a draft does none of the three, cut it. Keep the weekly mix balanced; don't ship only product posts,
and don't ship only broad takes.

## 2. Hooks
A strong hook has **authority** (a number, a customer, a result), a **curiosity gap**, and **ICP
relevance** (independent agency owners, producers, CSRs, account managers). Keep it short: aim for
under ~12 words on line one. Never write a hook the body can't pay off.

The 3 hooks per draft must use **different templates** from this list:
1. **Don't make the same mistake**: "If you want [outcome], don't make the mistake [X] made. Here's what happened:"
2. **I know someone who…**: "I know a [role] who [impressive result]. [Surprising twist.]"
3. **Authority + big list**: "I've [unique credibility] for [ICP]. [N] [mistakes/tips] stopping [ICP] from [result]:"
4. **Metric with zero [expected effort]**: "[Result] with zero [thing they assume is required]." Only if provable.
5. **Story + failure + what I'd do now.**
6. **The customer told me…**: "When we onboarded this agency, they told us [X] was their favorite part."
7. **99% think X. They're wrong.** Use sparingly.
8. **We spent X; now it's [cheaper/free].**
9. **Contrarian goal**: "[Statement against the grain]. Let me explain." Must be justifiable.
10. **Hero vs. enemy**: "Most [group] do [bad thing]. I'd rather be [hero] who…"
11. **You're doing X right, still getting Y wrong. Here's what to do:**
12. **If I had to [result] by [deadline], here's how:**
13. **Every [ICP] wants to answer these [N] burning questions:**
14. **Borrowed authority**: "[Known brand + specific metric]. I broke down their [X]. [N] takeaways:" Max once every 2 weeks.
Also allowed: a **direct quote** from the call as the hook (with permission), and a **plain
statement of the surprising fact**.

After "see more", **re-hook**: tell the reader in one line what's coming.

## 3. Draft A: Story structure
1. Hook (the moment or the tension).
2. Setup: who, where, what they were trying to do. Concrete details (tool names, carrier names if
   allowed, what they typed).
3. Turn: the surprising thing that happened or was said.
4. What it means for the reader (one lesson, not three).
5. Close with a line that lands, or a question if it's a Know post.
Specific detail makes it believable. One story per post.

## 4. Draft B: Framework structure
1. Hook (promise a usable result).
2. Re-hook: "Here's the [N]-step [workflow/checklist] [who] uses:"
3. Numbered steps or points. Each point = a clear statement + enough detail to act on it + one
   detail only someone in insurance ops would know. Depth over breadth: 3–5 deep points beat 10 shallow ones.
4. Close: the one-line takeaway. CTA only for Trust posts.
Framework variants: step-by-step process, checklist, "N questions to ask", "N mistakes", "If I were
[ICP] trying to [result]", before/after comparison.

## 5. Formats that work right now (pick the angle before the template)
Customer story / "How [agency] did [specific result]" · Process to get a result · Industry take half
your peers will disagree with (only if you believe it) · Expert commentary on news (post within 24–48h) ·
Origin story · Career story + lesson · Monthly recap · Non-obvious use of a tool the ICP already uses ·
Data-backed insight (first-party numbers beat opinions) · Borrowed-authority breakdown.

## 6. Writing rules (Humanizer + mistakes to avoid)
- Plain words, short sentences, varied length. Active voice. Talk to "you".
- Cut filler ("it's important to note"), hedges when sure ("might", "could"), and redundancy.
- No marketing hype, no AI tells ("dive into", "unleash", "game-changer", "in today's fast-paced world",
  "here's the thing", "let that sink in"), no hashtags, no emojis, no semicolons, no asterisks.
- Starting with "And" or "But" is fine.
- Scannable but not a line break after every sentence. 120–250 words for Story, 150–300 for Framework.
- Media hierarchy (best → worst): photo with a face > team photo > candid photo > infographic >
  carousel > screenshot > no media. For Gaya product shots: real screen recordings only.
- No CTA on Know posts. Match the CTA to the funnel stage.
- Don't obsess over one post; the pattern over 90 days is what counts.

## 7. Posting habits worth reminding authors of (optional footer in the report)
Comment on ~15 ICP posts before and after posting. Reply to every comment in the first 60 minutes.
Repeating a topic weeks later is fine; nobody remembers.
